# Four Calibration Frames Recover Ninety Percent of the Transfer Bias: A BLINDRAW Cross-Scene Recalibration Test

Author: Guangyi Zhao
Date: 2026-11-13
Subject: Bloom-Counter (my own open-source project), the BLINDRAW area-median scale estimator, cross-scene reference transfer and recalibration, and the MED3 median vote
Environment: Linux x86_64 VM, synthetic scenes generated locally, estimators computed in-process. All data is simulated; the article says so wherever it matters.

## 1. Problem and background

BLINDRAW's reference value, 3718.5, was calibrated on the replication scenes (seeds 101/202/303/404). Carried unchanged to new scenes, it produced two differently shaped transfer curves in my 2026-11-05 test: on holdout set one, the RAW error shifted up by roughly +3% to +4% across the whole usable band (s=0.65: +3.6%, s=0.5: +4.0%, s=0.28: +4.3%), like a level drift; on holdout set two, the mid-band sat at only −0.4% to −2.2%, but s=0.8 carried a −26.64% bad point. The open question left then was whether a handful of frames from the new scenes could recalibrate that bias away, how much of it would come back, and what a bias-corrected RAW would do inside the three-estimator median vote (MED3).

This test was preregistered before any run (data/2026-11-13-raw-recalibration-prereg.md): two brand-new pools, T1 and T2 (four new scenes each, 209 and 211 targets), and calibration frames allowed at s=0.5 only — at a new deployment position, shooting one frame at a known distance is what this pipeline can actually do in the field. Recalibration comes in three sizes: frames from 1 scene (R1), 2 scenes (R2), or all 4 scenes (R4) of the pool; plus a per-scene variant where each scene sets its own reference from its own frame. Six predictions were frozen and are scored in the text.

The headline, half of it against my preregistered intuition. The transfer bias is mostly a pool-level effect: on a new pool, not just RAW but all three estimators lean the same way in the healthy band, +3% to +7% — a common bias from how the scene pool was drawn, with RAW simply leaning furthest. Recalibrating from the pool's own four scenes (R4) pulls RAW's level bias back inside 0.3 points in both pools: by the preregistered accounting (mean signed error over the main band), T1 recovers 95.1% and T2 only 18.1% — T2's account is dragged down by a single fragmentation point at s=0.8, which recalibration does not fix and in fact moves from −20.7% to −25.0%; in the posthoc sensitivity that excludes that one point, T2's recovery is 95.4%. One-scene calibration (R1) is a lottery: in T1 it lands almost as well as R4, in T2 it is worse than not calibrating at all, because a single scene's own reference ranges from 3608 to 4320 (up to 13% away from its pool's value). The net effect on MED3 is far larger than I preregistered: the bad-point count stays 0 against 0, but MAE drops from 6.19% to 3.22% (T1) and from 4.57% to 2.03% (T2) — not because the recalibrated RAW becomes an accurate median, but because it drops out of the middle: in 13 of 15 steps (T1) and 8 of 15 (T2), the median's identity changes hands.

## 2. Method

- Estimators and definitions carried over unchanged: BLIND7 (kernel-7 morphology, contour areas, reference 3806.0), BLIND3 (kernel 3, reference 3740.0), BLINDRAW (raw-mask connected-component pixel areas, no morphology, reference 3718.5); every ŝ is sqrt(blind 2-means upper-cluster median / reference); MED3 is the median of the three; BAD is absolute relative error above 10%.
- New pools: T1 seeds 5111/5212/5313/5414, T2 seeds 6111/6212/6313/6414, all in the standard earlier composition (47 to 58 targets per scene), unused in any earlier article. Actual placed targets by generator ground truth: 209 and 211, matching nominal counts per scene, no placement losses.
- Recalibration: new reference = (pooled raw-mask upper-cluster median of the s=0.5 calibration frames) / 0.25, i.e. converted back to an s=1-equivalent area. Calibration scenes are the first 1/2/4 by ascending seed, frozen in advance. The s=0.5 step is the calibration step: R4's pooled error there is zero by construction, and the preregistered bias means exclude it; R1/R2 are partially circular there and are reported the same way. The main analysis band is the 12 steps s in 0.12 to 0.8 (11 after excluding the calibration step; the preregistration's parenthetical step count was a slip — the range definition governed the run and the accounting). s=1.0 (the known fragmentation step) and 0.10/0.07 are reported separately.
- A measurement-definition check, and one discarded run: the first version of this bench mistakenly computed RAW areas with cv2.contourArea instead of connected-component pixel areas. Its s=1.0 error came out at +5.6%, contradicting the roughly −55% fragmentation anchor published in every earlier article, and the run was discarded on the spot — pixel vs contour areas differ enough on small fragments to change the log-domain 2-means split entirely. The discarded data is kept with a -contourarea-invalid suffix; everything below is from the corrected rerun, whose s=1.0 RAW errors are −55.0% and −52.1%, matching the anchor.
- This run measures estimation error and MED3's BAD/MAE only, not end-to-end deployment F1, for the same reason as the last few articles: the deployment effect of ŝ changes was already measured on 2026-11-05, and no deployment benefit is claimed here.

## 3. Measurements

### 3.1 The new references, and how far single scenes scatter

| Pool | Old reference | R1 (1 scene) | R2 (2 scenes) | R4 (4 scenes) | Per-scene own references |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| T1 | 3718.5 | 4226.0 | 4174.0 | 4280.0 | 4120.0–4718.0 |
| T2 | 3718.5 | 3608.0 | 3766.0 | 4152.0 | 3608.0–4320.0 |

Both pools' full calibration references sit 11.7% to 15.1% above the old reference in area terms, which means the old reference overstates s by about 5.7% to 7.3% — the same magnitude as the old bias measured below, as it should be. The per-scene scatter is a separate account: within one pool, the highest and lowest own references differ by 14.5% (T1) and 19.7% (T2), and a single scene's own reference can sit 13.1% below its pool's value (T2's scene 6111: 3608.0 against the pool's 4152.0). That scatter explains everything about R1's lottery behaviour below.

### 3.2 The structure of the old bias: mostly common, RAW largest

On the main band (s 0.12–0.8, calibration step excluded), the old reference's pooled RAW bias is +6.91% on T1 and +3.04% on T2, 3.87 points apart. In the healthy band alone (s≥0.35), all three estimators lean the same way: T1 has BLIND7 at +5.26%, BLIND3 at +4.90%, RAW at +6.73%; T2 has BLIND7 at +3.28% and BLIND3 at +3.68% (RAW's mean there is pulled to +0.49% by the s=0.8 fragmentation point, MAE 8.78%; see 3.4). So most of the transfer bias is not "RAW's reference is stale" — it is that a new scene pool's bloom-size distribution differs from the calibration pool's as a whole, and three estimators sharing the same scenes lean together. This is the pooled version of the per-scene calibration bias that divergence could not see on 2026-11-12. RAW is special in only two ways: it leans furthest (healthy band +6.73% against +4.90%), and it has a fix that can be executed in the field — replace its reference. BLIND7's and BLIND3's references also come from the old pool, but the preregistration froze the intervention to RAW alone; their bias stays in place, which is exactly why MED3 improves the way it does in 3.5.

### 3.3 The recovery account

Recovery rate, defined up front: 1 − |post-recalibration bias| / |old bias|, with biases as the preregistered main-band signed means.

| Pool | Accounting | Old bias | R1 | R2 | R4 | R4 recovery |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| T1 | preregistered | +6.91% | +0.28% | +0.91% | −0.34% | 95.1% |
| T2 | preregistered | +3.04% | +4.60% | +2.39% | −2.49% | 18.1% |
| T1 | posthoc: also excl. s=0.8 | +7.14% | +0.50% | +1.13% | −0.12% | 98.3% |
| T2 | posthoc: also excl. s=0.8 | +5.41% | +7.02% | +4.75% | −0.25% | 95.4% |

T1 first, because its recovery is clean: after R4, 10 of the 11 main-band steps sit between −0.67% and +0.37%, the largest residual being −2.55% at s=0.8 (band MAE 0.45%). R1 and R2 also press the bias under one point — but that is not evidence that one frame is generally enough: T1's lowest-seed scene (5111) has an own reference of 4226.0, only 1.3% from the pool value 4280.0. It drew a good ticket.

T2 is the counterexample, twice over. First, R1 is worse than the old reference: the lowest-seed scene, 6111, is a small-bloom scene (its own per-scene bias under the old reference is −3.13%), so calibrating the whole pool from its frame yields 3608.0 — below even the old reference — and the pool bias rises from +3.04% to +4.60% (to +7.02% in the posthoc accounting). Second, R4's preregistered recovery is only 18.1%, and that is not a failure to recover the level: excluding s=0.8, R4's remaining 10 steps sit between −1.33% and +0.43% (posthoc bias −0.25%, recovery 95.4%). The entire shortfall is one step: at s=0.8 the old reference already reads −20.71%, a partial-fragmentation bad point of the same family as holdout two's; enlarging the reference by 11.7% moves it to −24.96%. The preregistered mean mixed a level shift and a single-step fragmentation event into one number, so P2 is scored as missed as written; split into components, the level component recovers over 90% in both pools and the fragmentation component recovers nothing, because it was never about the reference (see 3.4).

Per-scene self-calibration: all 8 scenes improve (main-band MAE, under both the including- and excluding-calibration-step accountings, 8/8). The worst old scene, T1's 5414 (old MAE 11.84%, bias +11.84%), drops to 0.82% self-calibrated. T2's 6111 improves from 3.36% to 2.14% — which does not contradict its failure as the pool's R1 representative: it is accurate for itself and 13% small for its pool. Self-calibration leaves one systematic tail: 7 of 8 scenes end with a negative self-calibrated bias (−0.71% to −2.87%, roughly −2%) — anchored at s=0.5, the curve drifts slightly downward toward smaller s. This run does not isolate the cause (Section 5).

### 3.4 The two steps no reference can fix

At s=1.0, the old reference reads −54.97% (T1) and −52.13% (T2); under R1/R2/R4 every value stays between −51.41% and −58.03%. Fragmentation crushes the upper-cluster median to fragment scale, and changing the reference merely translates a wrong reading. T2's s=0.8 point is the lightweight version of the same family (partial fragmentation, −20.71%), while T1's s=0.8 is a normal +4.54% — with one pool each way, this run cannot state the conditions under which 0.8 fragments, only log the risk: after recalibration, the largest residuals still come from the fragmentation family, not from level error.

### 3.5 The net effect on MED3: an identity change, not a better RAW

| Pool | MED3 MAE, old (main band) | MED3 MAE, R4 | BAD (30 points total) | Median identity changes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| T1 | 6.19% | 3.22% | 0 → 0 | 13/15 steps |
| T2 | 4.57% | 2.03% | 0 → 0 | 8/15 steps |

In the failure band, the old MED3's median identity is RAW (on T1, all six steps from s=0.30 to 0.15), sitting there with its +7%-class level bias, so MED3's error equals RAW's error (+7.20% at s=0.25). After R4 pulls RAW back to zero, RAW falls to the bottom of the three and the median identity in those steps passes to BLIND3 (at s=0.25, MED3-R4 reads +2.80%, BLIND3's own value). The improvement therefore does not come from "one of three votes got more accurate and the average improved" — a median has no average — it comes from a reordering: the repaired vote leaves the middle and the next-best vote takes its place. The implication cuts both ways: if the recalibrated estimator starts as the lowest of the three, recalibration lifts it into the middle and MED3 moves with it, for better or worse depending on its position relative to the old median. This run only measured the favourable direction; "recalibration improves MED3" must not be read as a general claim.

### 3.6 Preregistration scorecard

P1 (the two pools' old biases differ by at least 1.5 points, at least one pool at least 2%): hit — 3.87 points apart, at +6.91% and +3.04%. P2 (R4 recovers at least 60% in both pools): missed — T1 recovers 95.1%, T2 only 18.1% under the preregistered mean, held down by the s=0.8 fragmentation point; the posthoc split shows the level component recovering 95.4% on T2 as well, but the preregistered accounting is the accounting, and it is scored as missed. P3 (R1 differs from R4 by at least a point in at least one pool, or overshoots): hit — on T2, R1's bias is +4.60% against R4's −2.49%, a 2.11-point gap in |bias|, and R1 is worse than the old reference; T1 is the counterexample (R1 and R4 differ by 0.06 points), and both sides are on the record. P4 (per-scene self-calibration improves at least 5 of 8 scenes): hit — 8/8. P5 (MED3 BAD does not increase and the MAE difference stays under 0.5 points): missed — BAD is indeed 0 against 0, but the MAE differences are 2.97 and 2.54 points; I bet the net effect almost an order of magnitude too small, mechanism in 3.5. P6 (s=1.0 stays below −40% under every reference): hit — the range is −58.03% to −51.41%.

## 4. Analysis and trade-offs

Deployment conclusions, in order of evidence strength. First: on moving to a new site, recalibrate before reaching for any other guard. One known-distance frame (s=0.5-equivalent) from each of the pool's four scenes, the RAW reference replaced by the pool's own value, and the level bias falls from the +3% to +7% class to within 0.3 points (level component only; fragmentation steps excluded, fourth point below). It is the cheapest step in this pipeline with the largest recovery — provided the frames come from the pool itself and never from a single scene. Second: calibration quantity has a floor. One scene's own reference can sit 13.1% from its pool's value, so single-frame calibration stakes the whole pool on one scene's draw; two scenes (R2) got the direction right in both pools here with 0.9 to 2.4 points remaining; four scenes closed the level account. A harder floor needs the per-scene calibration sampling-error run already in the topic pool; this run only brackets it as "1 is not enough, 4 is". Third: the benefit spills into the vote. When the failure band's median identity happens to be the calibrated estimator, MED3 gains with it (here, 2.5 to 3.0 MAE points); but the gain depends on the ordering structure, so a deployment should check the median-identity account before counting on it. Fourth: after recalibration, the dominant residual is the fragmentation family (all of s=1.0, occasionally s=0.8), and guard attention should move from level to fragmentation detection (for instance, an anomalous RAW component count: at s=1.0 the raw component count runs far above the true object count). That is another line's problem; this run only names it.

Two things I deliberately did not do. I did not also recalibrate BLIND7/BLIND3 and report a "fully calibrated MED3": the preregistration froze a RAW-only arm, and adding arms after the run is posthoc design expansion. From the common-bias structure in 3.2, full calibration is where the largest expected gain sits (all three references replaced by pool values should erase the healthy-band bias wholesale), but it needs its own preregistered round — in particular, whether changing BLIND7's reference moves its failure-band shape. And I did not remove T2's s=0.8 point from the main accounting and re-score P2: a missed preregistration is a miss, and a sensitivity analysis explains it without overturning it.

## 5. Limitations and open problems

- Four scenes per pool, two pools: R4's recovery was measured with calibration scenes and measured scenes in the same pool. Cross-pool calibration (site A's frames setting site B's reference) would, by the scatter in 3.1, mostly swap one pool's bias for another's; it was not measured and must not be assumed.
- The trigger conditions for T2's s=0.8 fragmentation point are not isolated: across runs, that step has now fragmented on holdout two and T2 and behaved on the replication set, holdout one and T1 — three against two is not enough to state conditions. It is also the direct cause of P2's preregistered miss.
- The roughly −2% systematic negative bias after per-scene self-calibration is unexplained. One data-compatible reading is that RAW's upper-cluster median grows more slowly than s² (fragment merging differs at small s), but nothing in this run isolates it; it is recorded as unproven.
- Calibration uses a single step (s=0.5). If a field site can only shoot its known-distance frame at another s (say 0.8), the calibration reference inherits that step's fragmentation risk; this design does not cover it. Whether multi-step calibration frames that cross-check each other are more stable is unmeasured.
- "One frame at known s" presumes a controlled shot in the field (known distance, or a known-size reference in frame). In this simulator s comes from the scaling parameter directly; when the field's own s carries measurement error, the recovery will be discounted by that error, unmeasured here.
- All evidence is still this simulator's portrait: the bloom-radius distribution, the upper-median principle and the fragmentation behaviour all come from the one Bloom-Counter bench, and none of these numbers may be carried to other estimators.
