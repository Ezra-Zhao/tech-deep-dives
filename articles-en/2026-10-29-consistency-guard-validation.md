# Divergence from 0.4% to 170%: Validating a Multi-Estimator Consistency Guard — Perfect on the Replication Set, Two-Thirds on Holdout

Author: Guangyi Zhao

## 1. Background

The 2026-10-25 article in this series closed with an unvalidated candidate signal. The setting is Bloom-Counter camera recalibration: after a camera move, the scale factor s is estimated from the median bloom contour area, and two blind estimators differ only in morphology kernel size — BLIND7 uses the detector's own kernel 7, BLIND3 uses kernel 3. In that article's pooled data their divergence drew a suspiciously clean line: at most 0.4% across the healthy band (s ≥ 0.35), jumping to +17% to +24% across BLIND7's failure band (s = 0.15–0.30, where its estimate is biased +12% to +16%), and +80% / +171% at the extreme scales. The obvious inference — if the two estimators almost never disagree, then a large disagreement is itself a usable guard, covering exactly the extreme scales where the separation guard had gone blind — is what this article tests.

The uncomfortable part first: half of that clean line was an artifact of the replication set. On the original four scenes the consistency guard is indeed perfect — all 9 bad estimates caught, zero false rejections. Move the same frozen thresholds to four scenes that never took part in calibration and it still catches every bad estimate, but it also rejects 5 good ones, for a pooled accuracy of 0.667. Three of those five false rejections sit at errors of +9.4%, +9.7%, and +9.6% — within 0.6 points of the line that defines "bad" — and under an equally defensible definition the false-rejection count drops from 5 to 1. The conclusion that survives is not "5% is a good threshold". It is that divergence is a graded risk signal, and pressing it into a binary gate makes the gate's scorecard absurdly sensitive to where the evaluator drew the label line. Two further results are firmer: the separation guard is formally dead as a standalone guard (it misses precisely the worst scales), and in this pipeline the consistency guard and the contour-count guard are almost the same guard — identical pooled decisions, 15/15, on both datasets — so stacking them buys nothing.

Setup is unchanged from the earlier articles: the colour-segmentation pipeline (baseline min_area = 120, bloom_area_min = 900, kernel 7), greedy centre matching with label agreement, micro-averaged; the routing rule of 2026-10-24 (s-hat ≥ 0.30 takes the cheap P route, 0.15–0.30 takes the U route, below 0.15 takes U plus a "move the camera" alert). Baseline sanity F1 is 1.000.

## 2. Method

**Pre-registration.** Every threshold was frozen in data/2026-10-29-consistency-guard-prereg.md before any new data was run, derived only from the published 2026-10-25 pooled signal. The evaluator's truth label: a BLIND7 estimate is BAD when its absolute relative error exceeds 10% (the 2026-10-25 tolerance sweep showed f = 1.1 mostly lossless and f = 1.25 broadly lossy, and the candidate signal had healthy errors ≤ 2.5% against failure errors ≥ 12.2%). The three guards: CONS rejects when |s-hat7 / s-hat3 − 1| > 5% (about 13× the healthy maximum divergence of 0.38%, 0.29× the failure-band minimum of 17.42%, and half the 10% materiality line — if two estimators disagree by more than half the materiality threshold, at least one is materially wrong); SEP rejects when BLIND7's cluster separation is below 0.8 (the 2026-10-25 pre-registered value, unchanged); COUNT rejects when BLIND7's contour count falls below two-thirds of the same scene set's count at s = 1 (210 at baseline for the replication set, threshold 140; per-scene, that scene's own s = 1 count). All OR and AND combinations were scored as well.

**Two datasets.** The replication set is the original four scenes (seeds 101/202/303/404, 210 objects) at the same 15 scales, scored pooled and per scene (60 decisions). It must be said plainly: for CONS this set is in-sample — the threshold's inspiration came from its pooled signal — so it can only verify reproduction and per-scene behaviour, not generalisation. The decisive evidence is a holdout set of four new scenes (seeds 505/606/707/808; 52/48/59/44 objects, 203 total) with calibration references unchanged (3,806.0 / 3,740.0) and every threshold, rule, and routing line untouched. Calibration frames coming from different scenes than the field frames is deliberate; that is how field calibration actually works.

**End-to-end.** Pooled only: a rejected estimate falls back to the U route, as in the earlier guarded variant. Two references are added: CONS+RESCUE (on rejection, route — and if P, parameterise — with s-hat3 instead) and ALWAYS-B3 (route with s-hat3 throughout). The metric is total deployment-F1 loss against oracle routing across the 15 scales. Separately, because F1 cannot see it, I score the alert: at the three scales with true s < 0.15 the system is supposed to tell a human to move the camera; I count how often each policy actually does.

**Reproduction check.** Before any analysis, this run's replication numbers were compared against the 2026-10-25 raw data scale by scale: s-hat7, s-hat3, U-route F1, and P-route F1 under oracle, BLIND7, and BLIND3 parameters — six quantities, 15/15 exact matches each. The pipeline under test is the pipeline that produced the candidate signal.

Data and code: data/2026-10-29-consistency-guard-bench.json, post-hoc analyses in data/2026-10-29-consistency-guard-posthoc.json, pre-registration and bench script under the same prefix, rerunnable in the repository virtualenv.

## 3. Measurements

### 3.1 Pooled guard accuracy: perfect replication, sensitivity-only holdout

With BAD as the positive class (TP = correctly rejected, FP = falsely rejected, FN = bad estimate let through, TN = good estimate let through):

| Dataset | Guard | TP | FP | FN | TN | Accuracy | Sensitivity | Specificity |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Replication (15) | CONS | 9 | 0 | 0 | 6 | 1.000 | 1.000 | 1.000 |
| Replication | COUNT | 9 | 0 | 0 | 6 | 1.000 | 1.000 | 1.000 |
| Replication | SEP | 6 | 0 | 3 | 6 | 0.800 | 0.667 | 1.000 |
| Holdout (15) | CONS | 4 | 5 | 0 | 6 | 0.667 | 1.000 | 0.545 |
| Holdout | COUNT | 4 | 5 | 0 | 6 | 0.667 | 1.000 | 0.545 |
| Holdout | SEP | 2 | 5 | 2 | 6 | 0.533 | 0.500 | 0.545 |

Three observations. First, CONS and COUNT make identical pooled decisions on both datasets. The mechanism is not mysterious: divergence exploding and contour count collapsing are two readouts of one accident — kernel 7 kills the bud contours, the lower cluster vanishes, 2-means starts splitting the bloom cluster (divergence up) while the total count falls from 210 to under 131 (count down). The combination results say the same: every OR combination containing CONS or COUNT reproduces the single-guard scorecard cell for cell, and every AND combination containing SEP inherits SEP's misses. Stacking correlated signals buys complexity, not information.

Second, SEP's misses are all at the extremes. On replication it lets through s = 0.12, 0.10, 0.07 — the scales with errors of +12.2%, +80.9%, +162.0% — because their separations are 0.833, 1.359, and 1.023, all at or above its own 0.8 line: the surviving contours are mutually scattered, so the split clusters look well separated. Holdout repeats the pattern (misses at s = 0.10 and 0.07, separations 1.537 and 1.115; sensitivity 0.500). A guard that goes quiet exactly when the guarded object is most thoroughly dead is not a guard. SEP is retired as a release condition; it can stay in the logs as a diagnostic field.

Third, the holdout's five CONS false rejections all live in the transition band, and their quality is suspect. The rejected-but-good scales (s = 0.30, 0.28, 0.22, 0.15, 0.12) carry BLIND7 errors of +9.4%, +9.7%, +9.6%, +4.9%, +6.2% with divergences of 9.6%, 9.8%, 11.4%, 9.2%, 13.7%. On replication there was a void between the healthy band (divergence ≤ 0.38%) and the failure band (≥ 17.4%); on holdout the healthy maximum is 0.8% but the transition band starts at 9.2%, and the error spectrum itself is continuous (absolute errors from 0.8% to 139.3%, with seven of fifteen scales packed between 4.9% and 11.3%). A post-hoc sensitivity analysis — declared as such, not part of the pre-registration — moves the BAD line through 5%, 9%, 10%, 12%: replication accuracy for CONS stays at 1.000 throughout (a real void makes the line's placement irrelevant), while holdout accuracy swings from 0.933 (at 5%: 8 caught, 1 false) to 0.533 (at 12%). So "the consistency guard only gets two-thirds right on holdout" is half a statement about the guard and half a statement about the arbitrariness of the label line. The guard did smell degradation at 9% divergence; the degradation simply had not yet climbed over the evaluator's line.

### 3.2 Per scene: small samples inflate false rejections; the one miss is a three-way coin flip

Per scene (60 decisions; each scene's 44–62 contours clustered alone): replication CONS scores TP 27, FP 9, FN 0, TN 24 (accuracy 0.850), with COUNT's decisions identical 60/60 and SEP at TP 22, FP 9, FN 5 (0.767). Holdout CONS scores TP 19, FP 12, FN 1, TN 28 (0.783, sensitivity 0.950); COUNT scores TP 20, FP 16, FN 0 (sensitivity back to 1.000, more false rejections); the two agree on 55/60 decisions.

Every per-scene false rejection in both datasets (9 and 12) falls in the s = 0.12–0.30 transition band; the healthy and extreme bands have none. Small-sample clustering jitter and genuine incipient degradation stack in the same band, which is why the per-scene layer is a stress test, not a deployment unit — deployment pools frames, as in the earlier articles.

The run's only CONS miss deserves its own paragraph. Holdout scene 606 at s = 0.28: BLIND7 error +10.14% (0.14 points over the BAD line), divergence 4.83% (0.17 points under the CONS line), contour count 31 against a COUNT line of 32 (that scene's baseline is 48; two-thirds is 32 — COUNT does reject), separation 0.54 (SEP rejects clearly). Every quantity sits within a hair of its threshold, including the truth label itself. No guard malfunctioned; continuous degradation flipped a coin at the boundary. Any binary guard will produce such cases; the honest remedy is to mark boundary cases as borderline rather than pretend they do not exist.

### 3.3 End-to-end: F1 cannot rank these guards; the alert can

Deployment F1 is nearly boring, and is reported as measured: unguarded routing loses a total of 0.0169 on replication (a single scale, s = 0.28, F1 0.9831 against oracle 1.000 — s-hat7 = 0.3187 talks a U-scale into the P route) and 0.0025 on holdout (again only s = 0.28); every guarded variant, RESCUE, and ALWAYS-B3 lose 0. (At one holdout scale the U route beats oracle routing by 0.0025; that is the routing rule's own approximation error, not a guard's achievement.) The reason was already established in the earlier articles: routing is itself a shield — a bad s-hat in the 0.15–0.30 band mostly still lands on U, and the large raw P-route losses of 2026-10-25 (−0.687 at s = 0.07) never reach the deployment layer. On this bench, deployment F1 differs across policies only in the third decimal and cannot rank guards.

What the guards actually buy shows up in the alert ledger. At the three scales that truly need a "move the camera" alert (s = 0.12, 0.10, 0.07), the unguarded system alerts correctly once in each dataset: at s = 0.10 and 0.07 its s-hat7 is 0.1809 and 0.1834 (replication), 0.1977 and 0.1675 (holdout) — back above 0.15, so the system quietly accepts the frames on the U route and never mentions that the camera is too far away. CONS+RESCUE alerts correctly at all three scales in both datasets: after rejection it looks at s-hat3, which reads 0.1005 and 0.0678 (replication), 0.0995 and 0.0664 (holdout) at the extreme scales. Its price is one borderline false alert per dataset, exactly at s = 0.15, where s-hat3 reads 0.1397 and 0.1440 and slips over the 0.15 line — the same boundary disease as the Section 3.2 miss, on the other side. The U-fallback guards score nothing on this ledger at all: a rejection produces no replacement estimate, so the system knows it distrusts the number but has nothing to say about the camera. If the deployment cares about that alert, RESCUE is the only fallback with an output; if it cares only about F1, the fallbacks are indistinguishable on this bench.

The false-rejection cost also needs honest accounting. CONS rejects 9 of 15 pooled scales in both datasets, but a rejection only costs extra compute (the U route, at 5–33× the P route's cost per the 2026-10-24 timings) when the scale would otherwise have taken P. Scale by scale, rejections change the executed route at exactly two scales per dataset — s = 0.30 and 0.28. Every other rejected scale already had s-hat7 < 0.30 and was U-bound anyway; there the rejection only writes distrust into the log. On holdout those two route-changing rejections are false ones (errors +9.4%, +9.7%): insurance bought for two scales that could have taken P. On replication the same two are true catches (+13.3%, +13.8%). Reporting guard cost per rejection rather than per changed route would overstate the price roughly fourfold on this bench.

### 3.4 What a consistency guard cannot see: common-mode error

At s = 1.0 the holdout set returns s-hat7 = 1.023 and s-hat3 = 1.0246: on scenes they have never seen, both estimators are biased +2.3% and +2.5% together, with a divergence of −0.16%. That is scene-level variation in median bloom area — the calibration references come from four other scenes — and because both estimators share the same contours and the same references, they drift together. The rest of the holdout healthy band shows the same signature (s-hat7 errors +0.8% to +2.7%, all positive; divergence never above 0.8%). A consistency guard can only catch differential failure — one estimator dead, the other alive. When both are wrong together it is structurally blind. Here the common-mode bias is small (≤ 2.7%, well inside tolerance), but it marks the boundary of what this class of guard can promise: a clean divergence proves the estimators agree with each other, not that either is right.

Read together with Section 3.1, divergence is best used as a number, not a verdict: its Spearman correlation with |s-hat7 error| is 0.896 (replication) and 0.843 (holdout). It is a decently ranked risk score. The 5% threshold does not "discover errors"; it spends the U route's insurance premium on the scales with the highest risk scores — and on holdout, 7 of its 9 rejections correspond to errors above 9.4% or divergences above 9%, which is not a foolish way to spend it.

## 4. Analysis and Trade-offs

Deployment conclusions, in order of evidence strength:

1. **SEP as a standalone guard is finished.** Pooled sensitivity 0.667 and 0.500 across the two datasets, with every miss at the most extreme scales and a clear mechanism (survivor scatter inflates separation). Keep it as a logged diagnostic; never as a release condition.
2. **For a BLIND7-class estimator — one that measures the calibration quantity with the detector's own morphology — pick one of CONS or COUNT, by what you have.** Already running a second kernel? CONS needs no baseline visit. Holding a trustworthy baseline contour count? COUNT works. They are the same signal in this pipeline; do not stack them — no OR combination beat the best single guard, and every AND combination was dragged down by its weakest member.
3. **Prefer RESCUE over a bare U fallback when the second estimator is itself trustworthy** (BLIND3's pooled error stayed within 9.1% across both datasets here, and its failure direction differs from BLIND7's). What RESCUE recovers is the alert semantics — invisible in F1 tables, decisive in the field, where the question is whether the system ever tells a human to move the camera. With an untrustworthy second estimator, the bare U fallback is the more honest choice.
4. **The necessary cold water:** on this pipeline, ALWAYS-B3 — simply never using BLIND7 — matches every guarded variant's deployment loss (zero) and gets the alerts right natively (s-hat3 never climbs back above 0.15 at the extreme scales). So this article does not prove "guard BLIND7" is the right design. It validates a more general pattern: when the primary estimator's failure is differential — some independent readout is still working — a consistency check buys back the alert and the observability at near-zero F1 cost, with its false-rejection cost concentrated in a few boundary scales. When you can replace the primary estimator, replacing beats guarding. Guarding is for the cases where you cannot: measurement code that must match the detector's morphology for compliance or reproduction reasons, or a black-box primary estimator where an independent readout is all you can attach.
5. **Do not ship the threshold as a constant.** The holdout transition band's divergences (9.2%–13.7%) sit well below the replication failure band (17.4%–24.1%), because holdout's BLIND7 degradation is itself gentler. The 5% line kept its sensitivity on both sets only because it sits far from both sides' healthy noise (≤ 0.8%). A pipeline that degrades faster or slower needs the number re-derived by the same pre-registration procedure, not copied.

## 5. Limitations and Open Problems

- All evidence comes from one simulator family. The holdout scenes are new seeds from the same distribution, not a new camera, new lighting, or real field images; the +2.5% common-mode reading is an indication of the lower bound of scene variation, not an upper bound — in the field it will be larger.
- The 10% BAD line is an evaluator's convention. SEP's verdict is robust to moving it; CONS's false-rejection count is not. Cross-study comparisons of false-rejection rates must carry the label line with them.
- Deployment F1 is saturated on this bench (the U route is too strong, and routing shields a layer before the guard), so the guard ranking rests on detection metrics, alert recovery, and changed-route counts rather than F1. A deployment with a weaker U route, or no routing layer, could rank differently; untested.
- COUNT's per-scene variant used each scene's own s = 1 contour count, an evaluator's convenience; a field version needs a stored baseline count per camera, whose ageing is unmeasured.
- Per-scene samples hold only 44–62 contours, so clustering jitter dominates per-scene false rejections; that layer is a stress test, not a deployment basis.
- Three open problems, all queued in the topic pool: a two-tier guard (warn at a low divergence line, reject at a high one) and a quantitative divergence-to-error map — can boundary cases be flagged as borderline on holdout instead of coin-flipped?; a three-estimator median vote including BLINDRAW, whose failure lives at the opposite end (fragmentation at s = 1.0) — can a median survive both ends?; and what independent readout could guard BLIND3 itself, for which this run has no answer.
