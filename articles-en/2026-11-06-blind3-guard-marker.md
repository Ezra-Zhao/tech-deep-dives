# When All Three Votes Are Wrong Together, Who Raises the Alarm? A Known-Size Disc Guards the Median Vote's Common-Mode Blind Spot

Author: Guangyi Zhao

## 1. Problem and background

The previous article (2026-11-05) settled on replacing the calibration estimator with a three-estimator median vote, MED3: zero bad estimates across 45 pooled points, a perfect alert record. But it left one debt unpaid. A median protects against one vote dropping out; it does nothing when all three votes are wrong together. The evidence came in two places: at s=1.0 on holdout set 1, all three estimators shared a +2.3% scene-level bias; and in per-scene terms, the only 3 bad estimates in the whole study came from a single scene, #606, where all three votes were off by +10.1% to +11.5%. Worse, every guard that only looks at relationships between estimators (consistency checks, two-tier divergence guards) is structurally blind to this failure: three votes wrong in agreement produce zero divergence, and the guard sees a picture of health.

Guarding against it requires a reading that does not come from the scene's own distribution. This article does that work in the order the series has settled into: inventory the candidate signals first, pre-register, then validate. Four candidate second measurement principles went into the inventory, and two were rejected by the inventory itself — the rejection evidence is worth stating first:

- **Spacing (SPACE)**: infer s from the median nearest-neighbour distance between contour centroids. In the healthy band it barely moves with s (within ±0.36% on the replication set), which looks promising. But its reading shifts with the scene (−5.2% to −5.7% on holdout 1, −5.7% to −6.8% on holdout 2), and on scene 606 its error is +10.3% to +11.0% — the same direction and magnitude as the three votes. The mechanism sits in the simulator's placement rule: any two flower centres must be at least the sum of their radii plus 8 pixels apart, so when objects grow, spacing is forced to grow with them. Spacing is not independent evidence; it is a shadow of size.
- **Width (WIDTH)**: infer s from the upper-cluster median of contour bounding-box widths. That is a linear reading of object size itself, same source as area; the injection arm below measures exactly how coupled it is.
- **Count (COUNT)**: the contour count only moves once contours start dying at s≤0.15. Far too coarse to see a 10% bias; kept as background only.
- **Known-size marker (MARKER)**: plant a disc of exactly 40 px radius, in petal pink, in a calibration frame, and read its area through the same measurement pipeline to infer s. Its size is given by construction, not drawn from any scene's distribution — the only one of the four structurally independent of scene-level common mode. The cost is explicit: the deployment frame (or a calibration frame at the same camera position and distance) must actually contain a known-size object.

The headline results. On the deployment reading (pooled, s≥0.22) the marker guard is clean: zero false flags across 40 healthy pooled points from four datasets; it catches all of 606's three bad points in the retrospective account, all of the per-scene bad points of a newly appeared "second 606" (scene 1616, nine bad points) in a fresh holdout set, and 9/9 of the constructed common-mode injections (whole-scene radii scaled by 0.85 / 1.15 / 1.30 — MED3 bad on 9/9, marker flags 9/9). By contrast, the spacing guard false-flags 7 healthy pooled points and shows −0.7% to +0.5% divergence on 606 (it sees nothing), and the width guard goes 0/9 on the injection arm (its reading moves with the injection). The marker guard's weakness is at the bottom of the scale range: below s=0.22 its own negative bias deepens past −4.9% and it false-flags five pooled points on the new holdout set — but those scales route to U anyway, so the F1 cost of a false flag there is zero. Its usable range is now measured: deployable as-is for s≥0.22; below that it needs bias correction first, which is the next topic in the queue.

## 2. Method

**Pre-registration.** The candidate inventory (exploratory, used only for selection and threshold-setting), the guard definitions and six predictions were frozen in data/2026-11-06-blind3-guard-prereg.md before the main bench ran. Frozen definitions: the guarded quantity is MED3 exactly as defined on 2026-11-05, calibration references unchanged; a bad estimate is an absolute relative error above 10%; guard divergence is D = ŝ_MED3 / ŝ_guard − 1, and |D| above 8% is a FLAG (handling as before: reject the estimate, fall back to U, alert for human review). The 8% line comes from the pre-registration arithmetic: the marker's inventoried negative bias (≤2.89% for s≥0.22) stacked worst-case against MED3's published healthy pooled error (≤4.29%) gives about 7.4%.

**The marker reading.** Five calibration-frame replicates (foliage backgrounds, seeds 9000–9004, centre jittered ±60 px); the disc colour is the simulator's PINK, inside the detector's petal mask; after the same INTER_AREA rescaling as the main pipeline, its area is measured from kernel-3 morphology contours and the median of five gives ŝ_marker. The reference area is the same five frames measured at s=1: 4912.0 (the geometric circle area is 5026.5; the 2.3% shortfall is the polygon-area underestimate, calibrated out by using the measured reference).

**Three validation arms.** (1) Retrospective: on the three old datasets (replication 210, holdout 1 203, holdout 2 209 objects), MED3 values are taken from the 2026-11-05 archive while all three guards are computed fresh — the marker is scene-independent and spacing/width had never been computed, so there is no room for post-hoc tuning. (2) New holdout set 3: seeds 1313/1414/1515/1616, 206 objects, never used in any previous article; MED3, all guards and end-to-end deployment computed fresh; baseline sanity F1 1.000. (3) Common-mode injection arm: the new holdout scenes with every object's radius multiplied by g ∈ {0.85, 1.15, 1.30} (placement rules and canvas unchanged, true s unchanged), at s ∈ {1.0, 0.5, 0.30} — nine pooled points. Generation reuses the simulator's private drawing functions with only the radius sampling changed, RNG call order identical to the original generator; all 206 objects placed at every g. The spacing and width references (97.38 and 78.0) are recomputed from the replication set at s=1 and match the inventory values exactly.

**Six pre-registered predictions.** P1: marker error within 3% for s∈[0.22,1.0], −4% to −6.5% at 0.18/0.15, negative bias of at least 8% at ≤0.12. P2: zero marker FLAGs on the old pooled sets at s≥0.22, and all three of 606's bad points flagged. P3: on holdout 3, pooled MED3 bad count zero, marker pooled FLAGs no more than 2 and only below s<0.22, and every per-scene bad point at s≥0.22 flagged. P4: on pooled healthy points the spacing guard flags more than the marker, width in between or worse. P5: in the injection arm MED3 bad 9/9, marker flags 9/9, spacing at most 5/9. P6: on holdout 3, the guarded deployment's total F1 loss differs from the unguarded one by at most 0.005.

Data and code: data/2026-11-06-blind3-guard-bench.json, post-hoc summary data/2026-11-06-blind3-guard-posthoc.json, pre-registration and bench script under the same prefix.

## 3. Measurements

### 3.1 The marker's own error: one flat negative-bias curve

The marker's relative error (%), across the 15 scales in order: 0.00, +0.20, −0.34, −0.60, −1.35, −1.83, −2.37, −2.71, −2.88, −3.05 (s=0.22), −4.89 (0.18), −5.87 (0.15), −9.42 (0.12), −8.60 (0.10), −17.14 (0.07). The shape is plain: at s≥0.25 the bias stays within −2.9% and deepens monotonically as s shrinks (the smaller the circle, the larger the polygon-area underestimate's share), crosses −4.9% at 0.18, and collapses to −17% at the extreme. The curve is systematic and reproducible scale by scale (the five replicates' area readings differ by under 1%), which is both why it false-flags at low s (Section 3.4) and why it can in principle be corrected — random noise cannot be calibrated away, a systematic bias can.

### 3.2 Retrospective: 606's three bad points — the marker catches all, spacing is blind

Across the old sets' pooled points at s≥0.22 (30 points, all MED3-healthy), the marker guard flags zero — the false-flag account at deployment reading is settled first. Then scene 606's three bad points, one by one:

| Scene 606 | MED3 error | D vs marker | D vs spacing | Spacing's own error |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| s=0.30 | +11.07% | +13.76% FLAG | +0.45% | +10.57% |
| s=0.28 | +10.14% | +13.22% FLAG | −0.10% | +10.25% |
| s=0.25 | +10.32% | +13.59% FLAG | −0.65% | +11.04% |

The spacing guard's behaviour here is the single most archivable moment of this study: it does not fail because its reading is noisy, but because its reading is itself wrong by over +10%, so the divergence against MED3 is compressed to within ±0.7% — two errors from the same source issuing each other a clean bill of health. This is the "signals built only on inter-estimator relationships are structurally blind to common mode" claim from the 2026-10-29 article, now measured rather than asserted: the blindness is not a small divergence, it is a divergence of exactly zero.

### 3.3 Holdout set 3: pooled still spotless, and a second 606 appears per-scene

Pooled first: MED3 bad count 0, maximum error 8.50% (at s=0.10), mean absolute error 1.99% — across four datasets the pooled MED3 total is now 60 points with zero bad estimates. For contrast, on the same set BLIND7 is bad on 8 pooled points (the failure band at +14.5% to +16.1%, +145.6% at s=0.07) and BLINDRAW on 2 (−52.2% at s=1.0, −20.2% at s=0.8).

Per-scene there is a discovery: of 60 points, MED3 is bad on 10, and 9 of them belong to the new scene 1616 (seed 1616, 30 blooms and 16 buds), with errors of +10.06% to +11.32% spanning s=1.0 down to 0.10 — the same signature as 606: not a scale failing, but a scene whose objects are globally oversized, pulling all three votes by about +11% at every scale. The tenth bad point is scene 1414 at s=0.12 (+11.42%). The marker guard flags all 10 (divergences +10.68% to +23.0%). Common mode is not a 606 anecdote: two such scenes (606 and 1616) have now appeared across four datasets, and they account for the bulk of all per-scene bad estimates (the 1414 low-scale point, mechanism undetermined, is not counted in that claim). Pooled, the effect is diluted into invisibility by the other scenes; per-scene, it is the only enemy that matters — and it now has an alarm.

The false-flag account, reported plainly: per-scene at s≥0.22 (40 points), the marker flags 12 times on holdout 3 (6 true bad points, 6 false) and 9 times on holdout 1 (3 true, 6 false), and zero times on the replication set and holdout 2. Per-scene MED3 healthy errors themselves reach about 9%, and stacked against the marker's negative bias they cross an 8% line unsurprisingly. The operating conclusion is a division of readings: the guard is deployable at pooled reading (zero false flags there); per-scene it is an alarm reference with roughly a one-in-seven false rate, not a basis for automatic rejection.

### 3.4 The low end: five FLAGs, all below s=0.22, at zero F1 cost

On holdout 3 pooled, the marker flags 5 times, all at s = 0.18/0.15/0.12/0.10/0.07 (divergences +8.35% to +22.24%). The cause is two-sided: the marker's own bias deepens to −4.9%…−17.1% on those scales while MED3 on this set leans positive at the low end (+1.3% to +8.5%), and the divergence is pulled open from both sides. All five are false flags (pooled MED3 has no bad point there), but the handling cost happens to be zero: those scales route to U anyway (ŝ<0.30 goes U, <0.15 also alerts), and the FLAG handling "fall back to U" coincides with the original route — guarded and unguarded total deployment F1 loss on holdout 3 are both 0.0000. Zero cost on this bench does not mean ignorable: under a different routing boundary a false flag becomes a real cost, and since the bias curve is systematic, the right fix is correction that pushes the valid floor below 0.22, not a wider threshold.

### 3.5 Injection arm: build the common mode, and the three guards show their colours

Natural data contains too few pooled common-mode samples (pooling dilutes them all), so this arm is constructed: every radius in the scene multiplied by one constant g, so the three area votes should all err by about g−1 by construction. Measured MED3 errors: −13.90% to −15.34% at g=0.85, +14.78% to +16.14% at g=1.15, +30.70% to +32.77% at g=1.30 — bad on 9/9, the construction working as intended. The interception account:

| Guard | g=0.85 (3 pts) | g=1.15 (3 pts) | g=1.30 (3 pts) | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Marker | 3/3 | 3/3 | 3/3 | 9/9 |
| Spacing | 1/3 | 3/3 | 3/3 | 7/9 |
| Width | 0/3 | 0/3 | 0/3 | 0/9 |

Width's 0/9 is the textbook shape of coupling: its divergence against MED3 stays within −3.9% to +0.7% throughout, because the width estimator is reading the very size the injection scaled — when guard and guarded are wrong identically, divergence is identically zero. Spacing is partially coupled: at g=1.15/1.30 its reading only follows by +4.0% to +6.4% (the +8 px constant in the placement rule dilutes the radius change), leaving a divergence above +10% that still flags; at g=0.85 it follows down to −6.7% to −7.0% (shrinking makes the constant's share larger and the coupling tighter), the divergence is eaten down to −7.4% to −9.3%, and it misses 2 of 3. The marker's divergence tracks the injection almost one-to-one (+35.98% at g=1.30, s=0.30): it never looks at the scene, only at the disc, and no injection can reach it.

### 3.6 Pre-registration scorecard

Six predictions: P1 half — the 0.18/0.15 and ≤0.12 bands land exactly, but the s∈[0.22,1.0] band misses at s=0.22 with −3.05% against a 3% line, by 0.05 points. P2 full (zero pooled FLAGs on the old sets, all three of 606's bad points caught). P3 half — pooled bad count zero and the per-scene clean sweep both land, but pooled FLAGs were predicted at no more than 2 and came in at 5 (all below s<0.22: the location half of the prediction was right, the count half wrong). P4 half — spacing 7 flags against the marker's 0 lands; width "in between or worse" misses: on pooled healthy points width also flags 0, tied with the marker — judged on false flags alone, width would have passed, and only the injection arm exposes its coupling, which I did not foresee at pre-registration. P5 half — MED3 bad 9/9 and marker 9/9 land; spacing was predicted at no more than 5/9 and actually flags 7/9 (its coupling on the enlargement side is looser than I estimated). P6 full (guarded-minus-unguarded deployment loss difference 0.0000, inside the 0.005 allowance). Total: two full, four half — and three of the four halves are my predictions being conservative or wrong, recorded as such.

## 4. Analysis and trade-offs

First, the deployment decision on this bench: MED3's guard is the marker, the deployment reading is pooled, the valid range is s≥0.22. Three accounts hold simultaneously — interception (retrospective bad points 3/3, new-set per-scene bad points 10/10, injections 9/9), false flags (0 across 40 healthy pooled points), and cost (low-end false flags coincide with the U route, F1 cost zero). What it guards is precisely the class the median vote cannot: errors that come not from any vote failing but from the scene itself shifting all three votes together. The selection criterion generalises: ask where a reading comes from. Anything drawn from the scene's distribution — spacing, width, count, however different from area it looks — can be carried away by a scene-level factor. Only a reading whose size is fixed by construction, never sampled from the scene, earns the name independent.

Second, the false-flag account lies, and that is the most important methodological lesson of this round. The width guard posts zero false flags on every pooled healthy point, tied for first with the marker. Evaluated the conventional way — false alarms first, misses second — it would have passed alongside the marker. But its zero false flags and its zero interceptions are two faces of one fact: it is wrong in exactly the same way as the thing it guards, so it never argues and never alarms. A guard cannot be certified on healthy data alone; constructed sick data is a requirement, and the injection arm becomes a standing part of guard evaluation in this series from now on.

Third, spacing's partial coupling has an asymmetric detail worth keeping: it follows only a little on the enlargement side (still flags) and most of the way on the shrink side (misses). One unproven explanation: nearest-neighbour distance is dominated by object density (the injection changes neither count nor canvas, so density is fixed), and radius enters only through the placement rule's minimum separation — shrink the radii and the relaxed constraint lets near pairs get closer, so the reading does fall; enlarge them and most near pairs already sit far above the lower bound, so tightening it cannot move the median. Proven or not, the lesson stands: apparently unrelated signals get quietly coupled by the generating rules, and any reading grown from the same data must have its coupling paths audited before its independence is believed.

Fourth, the enemy's portrait is now complete. Common mode lives at scene level (one whole scene's objects oversized or undersized), is diluted to invisibility at pooled reading, is the dominant source of per-scene bad estimates (of the 13 per-scene MED3 bad points in this series to date, 12 come from scenes 606 and 1616; the remaining one is 1414's low-scale point), and is immune to every internal-consistency signal. The marker guard turns it from invisible into alarmable — but an alarm is where its competence ends: handling remains fall back to U plus human review. The marker tells you the three votes are off, not what the true s is (at low scales it is biased itself). Feeding the marker reading into the vote as a fourth vote is a different question, not tested here and not part of this article's conclusions.

## 5. Limitations and open questions

- The marker here is a calibration-frame construct: five replicates, median taken, backgrounds separate from the main scenes. A real deployment has one marker inside the frame, and a single per-frame reading's noise (background texture, partial occlusion, lighting) will exceed this study's five-replicate median. Whether the 8% threshold needs widening under a single-marker reading is unmeasured — queued as a follow-up topic.
- The marker's low-end negative bias parks the guard's valid floor near s=0.22. The bias curve is systematic and reproducible; how far correction can push the floor (0.15? 0.12?), and whether correction opens new misses in the injection arm, is unmeasured — queued as a follow-up topic.
- The injection arm's common mode is one idealised shape — every radius in the scene times one constant, modelled on the 606/1616 pattern. A real scene's size bias might act on blooms only or buds only (class-level common mode); the marker stays independent and the headline should hold, but the three votes' and the coupled guards' response shapes would differ. Unmeasured.
- The per-scene false-flag rates (6/40 on the new set, 6/40 on holdout 1) say a single-scene deployment that wants automatic rejection must re-calibrate the threshold against per-scene noise. This round only establishes pooled-deployable, per-scene-alarm-only; no per-scene optimal threshold was measured.
- All evidence still comes from one simulator family: the marker's colour is the simulator's petal pink, the backgrounds simulated foliage. In a real field a marker fades, collects dust, gets half-covered by leaves — noise sources for its own reading that this bench cannot expose.
- The queue continues: marker low-end bias correction, single-marker noise and threshold re-calibration, class-level common-mode injection — all three are in the topic pool.
