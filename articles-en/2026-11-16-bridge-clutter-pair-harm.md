# One Disc Bridges Two Flowers, and Both Disappear: The Compound Harm of Bridge Clutter, Measured

Author: Guangyi Zhao

## 1. Problem and background

In the 2026-11-07 test I moved a single petal-coloured disc toward one true flower, gap by gap. The harm curve was valley-shaped: pressed deepest, the disc did almost nothing; the peak sat at the shallow-touch gaps, and the harm had a single shape — a bud whose merged area crossed the classification threshold and was relabelled a bloom. Not one flower was ever truly lost (lost stayed 0 throughout). That article left one question open: what happens when the disc does not lean on one flower, but sits between two, touching both at once and merging the pair into a single contour?

Intuition says it should be worse. A single-host merge can at most relabel one flower; a bridge merge forces two ground truths to share one detection, so the count is short before classification even enters the picture. How much worse, how it varies with the pair's spacing and the disc's size, and whether relabelling stacks on top — I had no data. This test measures exactly that (preregistered in data/2026-11-16-bridge-clutter-prereg.md, frozen before the main run, predictions P1–P6).

The headline results first. The bridging threshold obeys the same per-side law as the single-host case: with an edge-to-edge gap of about 0 px or less on each side, merging is near-certain; at 3 px or more per side it essentially stops; the two sides do not add up. In pair terms, the largest flower spacing a disc of radius rc can bridge is roughly 2rc plus 4 to 8 px (at rc=12, measured 24–28 px). Once a full merge happens, the harm comes in three types. A bud pair is the worst, a compound injury: in all 208 fully merged trials the merged contour's area crossed the 900 bloom threshold (minimum 974.0), and every one of the 186 trials in fully merged cells ended TP 0, FP 1, FN 2 — both buds gone, plus one phantom bloom reported. A bloom pair at narrow spacing only loses count (TP 1, FN 1), but at wide spacing a third failure mode appears: the merged detection's centre lands midway between the two flowers, farther than the matching radius from either, so the scoring rule assigns it to nobody — TP 0, FP 1, FN 2, exactly as bad as the bud pair, and caused by the evaluation rule rather than the detector. A mixed pair (bloom plus bud) is constrained by geometry: the bridge must first reach the bud side, so at rc of 12 or less it almost never fully merges; when it cannot reach, the disc settles for the bloom side alone, producing a wide half-merge band that turns out to be harmless — the bloom absorbs the disc and both flowers are still counted correctly.

## 2. Method

- Pairs were drawn with the simulator's own drawing functions (simulator/generate.py: _draw_bloom and _draw_bud) onto its own foliage backgrounds (_foliage_background, seeds 7001–7006, six images), with no JPEG round-trip. Radii are class representatives: bloom r=38 (simulator range 30–49), bud r=15 (range 11–16). Three pair types: bloom–bloom (BB), bud–bud (bb), bloom–bud (Bb).
- The bridge is a solid disc, pink (147, 20, 255), inside the detector's _petal_mask pink range and the same colour family as the 2026-11-07 clutter. Its centre sits at the midpoint of the two flower centres and it is drawn last, on top. Radius rc takes six levels: 0 (no-bridge control), 6, 9, 12, 15, 18.
- Spacing G is the edge-to-edge distance between the two flowers at nominal radii (centre distance d=r1+G+r2), at eleven levels: 0, 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24, 28, 32, 40, 48 px. Pair orientation rotates through horizontal, vertical and the two diagonals; the midpoint jitters within ±60 px of the image centre. Six replicates per condition (the six backgrounds in rotation): 3 pair types × 11 spacings × 6 radii = 1188 trials in the full factorial.
- The per-side bridging gap is the geometric quantity g_i=d/2−rc−r_i. For symmetric pairs both sides are equal, g=G/2−rc. For a mixed pair the bud side is g=G/2+11.5−rc and the bloom side g=G/2−11.5−rc; the bud side binds.
- Detection uses the detector's default parameters (min_area=120, bloom_area_min=900, morph_kernel=7, CLOSE then OPEN), the same pipeline as the rest of the series. By the contour method: one contour of area at least 120 containing both flower centres is a full merge (merged_both); a contour containing the bridge centre and one flower centre but not the other is a half merge (merged_one); each flower's correct / migrated / lost follows the 2026-11-07 definitions from its contour's label against its true label. The end-to-end account uses the series-standard greedy centre matching (labels must agree, distance at most max(38, 1.1r)). The backgrounds hold no other ground truth, so every detection in the image is attributable to the pair and the bridge.
- Same-run references: a lone bloom's contour area medians 3888.5, a lone bud's 453.0. A lone bridge disc measures: rc=6 not measurable (below the floor after opening, see Section 3.4), rc=9 → 222.0, rc=12 → 406.0, rc=15 → 664.0, rc=18 → 952.0.
- The single-host comparison is not re-run; it cites the published 2026-11-07 numbers: merge rate 100% at gap=-8, 84.8% at 0, 36.6% at 4, 0% at 8 and above, 50% crossing near 3.1 px; bud migration peaks at 14.3% at gap=-3; lost is 0 throughout.

## 3. Measurements

### 3.1 The threshold: about 0 px of bridging reach per side, and the two sides do not add

Pooling the symmetric pairs (BB and bb), the full-merge rate by per-side gap g:

| Per-side gap g (px) | −1 or less | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 to 5 | 6 or more |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Full-merge rate | 0.94 to 1.00 | 0.667 | 0.542 | 0.444 | 0.083 | 0.000 |

Three readings. First, the certain zone ends at g=−1 (0.94–1.00); at g=0 the rate is already down to 0.667 — exact tangency does not guarantee a merge, because mask-edge quantisation and petal shape turn the tangent level into a coin flip weighted two-to-one. Second, the transition band is fatter than the single-host curve: 0.54 and 0.44 remain at g=1 and 2, a 0.083 residue persists through g=3 to 5, and zero is reached only at g of 6 or more. The single-host curve went 84.8% (0) to 36.6% (4) to 0% (8 and above); the shapes are kin, but the bridge's tail is longer. One explanation is ready to hand (a mechanism reading, not separately isolated by measurement): g is computed at nominal radii, while a flower's actual mask reach is random — a bud is an ellipse at a random angle whose semi-axis along the pair direction varies between 0.55r and r, a swing of several pixels, and a bloom's petal tips vary by several pixels as well. At the same nominal g, the true mask gap can differ by about 7 px, so within one nominal level some trials are truly tangent and others are still pixels apart; shape randomness smears the transition band wide. Third, translated back into engineering units, the largest spacing G at which the full-merge rate is at least 0.5: at rc=6, BB 12 and bb 16; rc=9, 20 and 16; rc=12, 28 and 24; rc=15, 28 and 32; rc=18, 40 and 40. Roughly, G ≈ 2rc plus 4 to 8 px — beyond the disc's diameter, each side earns about 2 to 4 px of closing reach, the same magnitude as the single-host per-side value of about 3 px. There is no doubling: bridging is not "one disc's harm against two flowers added together". The threshold is set by each side's gap independently, and both sides must pass before a bridge exists.

The mixed pair verifies the same law on its binding side. Pooled by the bud-side gap, the full-merge rate is 0.83 to 1.00 at g of 2.5 or less, drops to 0.25 to 0.42 from g=3.5, and reaches zero at g of 6.5 or more. In maximum bridgeable G: at rc=6, 9 and 12 only G=0 gets over half (0.83); at rc=15 and 18 the limit is 16. Against the symmetric pairs at the same rc, the mixed pair bridges about 12 to 24 px less, the same magnitude as the 23 px the preregistration computed from the radius difference (inside the ±6 px tolerance).

### 3.2 Harm after a full merge: compound for buds, two regimes for blooms

Cell-by-cell accounting over the cells whose full-merge rate is 1.0 (BB 26 cells, bb 31, Bb 6):

| Pair | Trials in fully merged cells | TP distribution | Mean FP | Mean FN | Median merged area | Share of merged areas ≥ 900 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| BB (bloom–bloom) | 156 | TP=1 in 72, TP=0 in 84 | 0.54 | 1.54 | 7787.0 | 100% |
| bb (bud–bud) | 186 | TP=0 in 186 | 1.00 | 2.00 | 1370.2 | 100% |
| Bb (bloom–bud) | 36 | TP=1 in 36 | 0.00 | 1.00 | 4414.8 | 100% |

(The trial counts and the TP, FP and FN columns cover the cells whose full-merge rate is 1.0; the merged-area medians and the at-or-above-900 shares cover every trial in which a full merge occurred — BB 209, bb 208, Bb 76 — including stray merges inside cells that did not fully merge.)

The bud pair is the cleanest and the worst type. Two buds of about 453 each, plus the disc (406 at rc=12), minus overlaps, give a merged area with median 1370.2 and minimum 974.0 — every merged contour crosses the 900 line and is labelled a bloom. Neither bud can match it (labels disagree), and the merged detection itself becomes a bloom-class false positive. Every single full merge books TP 0, FP 1, FN 2: the headline count is off by only −1, but the structural error is 3. This is the same account that 2026-10-17 and 2026-10-21 kept measuring: the total looks almost right while the class structure is entirely wrong.

The mixed pair fully merges in only six cells (rc=15 and 18, G of 16 or less), and the outcome is uniform: the bloom is counted through the merged detection (TP 1), the bud is lost (FN 1), no false positive. The bud is carried across the line by the bloom's area; inside the merged contour its own label has no vote.

The bloom pair splits at G=16. In the narrow cells (fully merged at G=0) the loss is pure counting: TP 1, FP 0, FN 1 — the merged detection falls inside one flower's matching radius, one flower is counted, one is lost. G=8 is transitional (mostly TP 1, some TP 0). In every fully merged cell at G of 16 or more (several cells at rc=9, 15, 18) the result is uniformly TP 0, FP 1, FN 2: the merged contour's bounding-box centre sits at the pair's midpoint, d/2 away from each flower centre, and at G=16, d=92, so the half-distance is 46 px — beyond the matching radius max(38, 1.1×38)=41.8 px. The detector did report one very large "bloom" of area above 7000; the scoring rule assigned it to nobody. This type of harm lives in the evaluation rule, not the detector — but any deployment that acts on detection centres (a robot arm sent to pick, a human sent to verify) meets the same ownerless detection, so the account belongs to the system, not only to the benchmark.

### 3.3 The half-merge band: mixed-pair bridges mostly hold only the bloom, and that is harmless

When a full merge fails, where does the bridge go? For the mixed pair, the half-merge rate is exactly 1.0 across wide regions: at rc=6 for G=4 through 32, at rc=12 for G=16 through 48, at rc=15 and 18 for G of 20 and above — always the bridge holding one flower only. Reading the contour areas shows it is always the bloom side: in the Bb, G=48, rc=12 trials, the bloom's contour measures 4297.5 to 4342.5 (lone value 3888.5 plus the disc's 406, give or take), while the bud's contour measures 451.0 to 464.0, indistinguishable from its lone value. The bloom's petal ellipses reach outward, so its actual mask edge sits about 4 px beyond the nominal radius; a disc at the midpoint reaches the bloom first.

And it is harmless: in those half-merge cells both flowers end correct, with FP and FN both 0 (spot-checked at G=20/rc=6, G=32/rc=6, G=48/rc=12, G=24/rc=15 and G=40/rc=18 — 6 of 6 double-correct in each). The disc is absorbed by the bloom: it neither drags the bud in nor becomes a false positive of its own. Symmetric pairs show the same shape in their transition bands (for example BB at rc=6, G=16 and 20 has half-merge rate 0.667): the bridge sticks to one flower, the other stays independent, and the harm falls back to at most the single-host grade — the stuck-to flower may migrate, nothing amplifies.

### 3.4 The no-bridge control, and the disc's own account

The rc=0 control bounds how much the two flowers can merge on their own: at G=0 the direct merge rate is BB 1.0, bb 0.333, Bb 0.5; at G=4, BB 0.667, bb 0.5, Bb 0; at G of 8 or more the intact rate (both flowers correct and unmerged) is 1.0 for BB and bb, while Bb has one merge in six at G=8 (intact 0.833) and is at 1.0 from G=12 on. Without a bridge, two flowers must come within about 4 px edge-to-edge before merging is even a topic, and beyond 8 px they are essentially safe. The disc stretches that danger band from 4 px to roughly 2rc plus 4 to 8 px — that widening is the bridge's entire contribution.

The disc's own account, when it touches nothing, comes in two sizes. An rc=6 disc is not measurable alone: its nominal area is about 113, already at the 120 floor, and after opening its contour falls below the floor — in every no-merge cell its FP contribution is exactly 0; morphology eats it. Discs of rc=9, 12 and 15, whenever they touch no flower, duly report one bud-class false positive each (mean FP 1.0 across the no-merge cells). An rc=18 disc measures 952.0 alone, already over 900 — it is a phantom bloom by itself and needs to bridge nobody. This is the same floor account as 2026-10-30: once clutter is big enough to cross the classification threshold on its own, its harm changes species, from merge-and-relabel to standalone false alarm.

### 3.5 Preregistration scorecard

- P1 (symmetric threshold: full-merge rate at least 90% at g of 0 or less, at most 10% at g of 9 or more, 50% crossing inside g in [0, 6]): half right. The crossing sits near g=1 to 2, inside the preregistered band, and the rate is already down to 0.083 by g=3 — earlier than the g of 9 I bet on, both in my favour. But at g=0 the rate is only 0.667, not 90%: the certain zone really ends at g=−1. "Exactly tangent always merges" was wrong.
- P2 (mixed asymmetry: the bud side binds, threshold G ≈ 2rc−17 within ±6 px, about 23 px less reach than symmetric pairs at the same rc, half-merges dominate the in-between band): hit. At rc=15 and 18 the maximum bridgeable G is 16 against formula values 13 and 19, both inside tolerance; at rc of 12 or less only G=0 gets over half; the half-merge band sits at rate 1.0 over wide regions.
- P3 (bb full merges: at least 90% of merged areas at or above 900, outcome TP 0, FP 1, FN 2): hit, and more uniform than bet — the area share is 100% (minimum 974.0), and all 186 trials in fully merged cells end with exactly that outcome.
- P4 (BB full merges always TP 1 and FP 0; Bb full merges TP 1, FN 1, FP 0): half right. The Bb part is exact (36 of 36). The BB part holds only at narrow spacing; at G of 16 or more every fully merged cell flips to TP 0, FP 1, FN 2, because the merged detection's centre falls outside the matching radius. In the preregistration I had silently assumed a merged detection is always claimable by one of its flowers; at wide spacing that assumption fails.
- P5 (no-bridge control: direct merges only at G of 4 or less, intact rate at least 90% at G of 8 or more; the lone-disc FP account): half right. BB and bb are intact at rate 1.0 from G=8, but Bb has one merge in six at G=8 (intact 0.833), just under the 90% line. The disc account is exact: rc=6 is eaten by opening, rc=9 to 15 report a bud-class FP, rc=18 is a bloom-class FP on its own.
- P6 (per-event harm: median FN of a full merge is 2 for bb and 1 for BB and Bb, against a median of 0 for single-host merges): half right. bb and Bb are as bet. BB's median FN is 2, not 1 — the extra lost flower is precisely the matching-radius effect P4 missed. The directional conclusion survives: a bridge full merge loses at least one flower per event where a single-host merge loses none and only relabels; the bridge is one grade heavier, just by a different path for bloom pairs than I had bet.

Three halves out of six, and the misses share one root: I treated "a merged detection" as a detection the matching rule can always claim. At narrow spacing that is true. At wide spacing the merged detection hangs between the two flowers, the claim fails, and the harm steps up another grade.

## 4. Analysis and trade-offs

Laying the three clutter accounts side by side, the full picture now has three layers. Layer one is the single-host merge (2026-11-07): relabel harm, peaking at shallow touch, worst case one bud becomes a bloom while the total stays right. Layer two is the bridge full merge (this test): a compound of lost detections plus relabelling — a bud pair loses two and pays a phantom bloom, a bloom pair at wide spacing loses two the same way. Layer three is the disc's own size (2026-10-30 and Section 3.4 here): big enough to cross 900 alone, it no longer needs to touch anyone.

Three judgements are portable to deployment. First, the danger band is computable: same-colour clutter between two true flowers can bridge at pair spacings up to about 2rc+8 px. Review effort should go first to frames where a same-colour connected mass appears between two flowers spaced under roughly 50 px — that is where bridges happen. Second, dense bud regions are the bridge's killing field: two buds sit far below the 900 line on their own (about 453 each), yet a bridged pair crosses it with certainty, booking a structural error of 3 behind a count error of 1. Any acceptance check that reconciles only totals will wave this type through entire; reconciliation must be per class — the same conclusion 2026-10-21 reached from the evaluation side, now confirmed from the clutter side. Third, the evaluation rule needs a claim path for merged detections: when scoring by centre distance, a detection whose area is far above the single-flower range (say, at least twice the single-bloom median area) should be allowed to book against multiple ground truths it covers. Without that path, a wide bridge is booked as one FP plus two FN — the same event fined two and a half times — and whoever maintains the detector will go hunting a false-positive problem that does not exist. This changes evaluation bookkeeping only, not the detector.

The harmlessness of half-merges deserves its own note. When the bridge holds only the bloom, the bloom's area grows from 3888.5 to about 4300 and nothing else changes — no label flips, no count moves. The switch for bridge harm is not "did the disc touch a flower" but "did it touch two at once". A review rule that alarms on abnormally large detections would report a crowd of harmless half-merges; a rule that alarms on "one detection covering two ground-truth centres" aims at full merges only. This is the same lesson the marker-guard line learned: aim the alarm at the binding mechanism, not at surface abnormality.

On cost: nothing here adds deployment machinery. The prescriptions are bookkeeping changes to existing review and evaluation — per-class reconciliation, a claim path for merged detections, bridge-band-first review — all rule edits, no extra compute.

## 5. Limitations and open problems

- Flower radii are fixed at representatives (bloom 38, bud 15); the radius dimension is not swept. When the two flowers differ in size, the smaller one binds (the mixed pair demonstrates this), but the threshold surface for a large–small pair of the same class is unmeasured. The bud radius of 15 is the top of its range: whether a bridged pair of r=11 buds (roughly 250 each, plus the disc) still crosses 900 is untested — P3's 100% is proven for r=15 only, and small-bud pairs may stay bud-class merges that lose count without paying the phantom bloom.
- The disc sits exactly at the midpoint. An offset bridge — deep on one side, shallow on the other — makes the two side gaps asymmetric, and the full-merge threshold should be set by the shallow side; the offset dimension and the half-to-full transition under offset are unmeasured. This is the natural extension of pool item 12: measure the threshold surface of an offset bridge.
- The disc is a solid circle of one colour. Real bridging material — a fallen petal stuck between flowers, a leaf edge, a same-colour fruit — is irregular, and its mask connectivity will differ from a disc's; the 2026-11-07 shape account (square clutter has a wider effective contour band) suggests shape moves the threshold, by an unmeasured amount.
- The matching-radius effect (the third harm type in Section 3.2) appears only on bloom pairs because only their centre distances grow large enough. What it means for a real deployment depends on whether that deployment associates detections by centre at all; this test books it under the series evaluation rule. Under IoU-based or coverage-based matching the ledger for that type would read differently — but the geometric fact, a merged detection hanging between two flowers, does not depend on the rule.
- All evidence is still this simulator: petal shapes, colours and backgrounds are synthetic, and the pixel numbers in the thresholds (about 3 px per side, danger band 2rc+8) belong to kernel 7 and this colour pipeline. Change the detector or the kernel and they must be re-measured. What transfers is the structure: the binding-side law, the harm typology, the harmlessness of half-merges, and the way total-only reconciliation lets structural error through.
